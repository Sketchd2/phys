Multiscale simulation · design note

The 1060 problem

A galaxy contains 1.2 × 1066 baryons. A consumer GPU can step around 106 bodies per frame at 20 updates per second. No optimisation closes that gap, and no hardware will. So the engine described here does not simulate a galaxy — it is built to be indistinguishable from one, and that is stated as a tested claim rather than a slogan.

The gap~1060

For scale: 1060 is forty-five orders of magnitude beyond what Moore's law has left. One atom of memory per particle would require more atoms than exist in 1037 galaxies. This is not an engineering problem with a clever solution — it is a category error to attempt directly.

The way out is to change the goal. Nobody needs a galaxy; they need something no observer inside it can distinguish from one. And indistinguishable is a finite requirement, because observers are finite — they have apertures, integration times, photon budgets, and at the bottom the uncertainty principle, which puts a hard ceiling on how much detail any region can be asked to have. "Simulate down to the subatomic level" turns out to be a bounded request.

So the specification becomes: for any observation an observer can physically perform, return the answer a real galaxy would have returned, within that observation's own error bars, in bounded time. That is achievable.

Structure

Seven regimes, twenty-three levels

A tier is a physics regime, not a level of the tree. Many refinements happen inside one tier; the tier changes only when the characteristic size crosses a boundary where a different description becomes appropriate. Conflating the two is a tempting simplification that gives seven refinement levels between a galaxy and a nucleus, when the mass ratio alone demands more than twenty.

Architecture

Four ideas

The engine is assembled from established techniques — adaptive mesh refinement, Barnes-Hut, sub-grid models, procedural generation, conservative discrete-event simulation. The gaps between them are consistent: the level-of-detail techniques are not conservative, the conservative techniques are not lazy, and none of them is driven by what an observer can actually see.

Materialisation

Detail is generated, then destroyed

Fine structure is sampled on demand from a seeded distribution and thrown away when nobody is looking. The sampler is constrained so that coarsening the generated detail returns the original bulk state exactly — energy, momentum, angular momentum, charge, baryon and lepton number.

Measured worst case across 126 configurations spanning 60 orders of magnitude in mass: 5.8 × 10⁻¹⁶. That is machine epsilon.

Causality

Light speed is the scheduler's lookahead

Conservative parallel discrete-event simulation needs a guarantee that no message will arrive before now + L. Obtaining one is the classical difficulty of the field. Here relativity supplies it free: a region whose nearest neighbour is at distance d has lookahead d/c, with no analysis and no annotation.

Two clouds a kiloparsec apart step independently for three thousand simulated years. Two nucleons 2 fm apart step in lockstep. Same rule.

Structure

Some matter has a history, not a temperature

A tree is not a sample from a distribution — it is the specific record of which branch got shaded in year three, and an observer who saw it yesterday will notice a different one. Max-entropy sampling is exactly the wrong generator.

So for ordered matter the generator becomes a developmental program run to an age. Measured: 104 bytes of state standing in for 6,000 rendered parts — 10,615× smaller, and the ratio grows with the structure because the state does not.

Observation

Measuring is committing

An unobserved quantity has no value; a measured one is recorded permanently and never re-sampled. Measure a decay time twice and you get the same answer, because the first measurement made it a fact.

At the subatomic tier this stops being a trick and becomes the physics. The approximation runs the other way — classical trajectories are the approximation.

Budget

Frames spend detail, not time

Each frame gets 50 ms and solves a knapsack over the available work, ranked by observer salience, causal urgency and the estimated error of skipping it.

Frame rate is the invariant; fidelity is the free variable. Zoom too far and you get a slightly coarser world with a visible detail-debt readout, not a slideshow.

The central mechanism

How invention is made conservative

Procedural generation and physical simulation are normally incompatible: generation invents, simulation conserves. Throw away a cloud's million parcels and regenerate them later, and the new set has different total energy and momentum — a difference that accumulates every time the camera moves.

The fix is to sample from the maximum-entropy distribution consistent with the coarse state, which makes it look right, then project exactly onto the constraint surface. The projection is ordered so each correction lives in the null space of the constraints already satisfied, and nothing is ever undone:

  1. Centre the positions Σ m r = 0Establishes the centre of mass the rest of the construction is measured against.
  2. Subtract the mean velocity Σ m v = 0Leaves the positions untouched.
  3. Subtract the rigid rotation Σ m r × v = 0Removing a rotation adds no net momentum, so the previous constraint survives.
  4. Scale the residual both still 0Scaling is linear, so a field with zero momentum and zero angular momentum keeps both.
  5. Add the target rotation L = L targetA rigid rotation about the centre of mass carries no net momentum.
  6. Add the bulk drift P = P targetA uniform translation adds no angular momentum about the centre of mass.

Because the residual field has zero angular momentum by construction, it is energetically orthogonal to the rotation added in step five — Σ m δ·(ω×r) = ω·Σ m r×δ = 0 — which is what lets the energy scale be solved in closed form instead of iterated.

Where the exactness actually comes from

Two refinements turn "very accurate" into "exact". Energy is closed algebraically, not by iteration: restriction computes internal energy as E_kin + Σ U − K_bulk, so Σ U is a free parameter that appears linearly and disturbs neither momentum nor angular momentum. Solving for it makes total energy exact for any configuration, including the awkward ones where the velocity projection converges poorly.

Angular momentum the geometry cannot carry becomes intrinsic spin. Two point masses have no moment of inertia about their own axis; the inertia solve correctly reports this as singular. The angular momentum still has to go somewhere, and the honest place is the children's own spin — which is exactly what the parent's spin was, one level down.

The orbit test earns its place. Retarding a source's position alone produces a tangential force that does net work on a bound orbit and unbinds it — Laplace's objection to finite-speed gravity. The physical field of a uniformly moving source points at its instantaneous position; the retardation is cancelled by the velocity-dependent part of the field, and what survives is the gravitomagnetic term. Fifty orbits is enough for the naive version to fail unmistakably.

Growth

Building order without breaking the books

Growth runs on the aggregate, never on the structure. A forest does not grow by integrating 10⁹ trees; it grows by advancing one ordinary differential equation on a forest node, at constant cost per node. That is cheap enough to run on the entire world every frame while the fine structure stays unbuilt — so the laziness the rest of the engine works hard for is simply free here.

Building order out of disorder costs free energy and exports entropy, so every step returns a transaction that is checked before it is applied. A program that tried to grow too efficiently is refused, not smoothed over.

The calibration that the second law caught

A leaf absorbs the entire solar flux and stores about 0.3% of it; the rest leaves as heat and infrared. Booking only the fraction that gets used — the obvious simplification, and the one that was written first — describes a perfectly efficient converter, and the validator rejected it. A device that turns all of its input into stored free energy while lowering its own entropy is not allowed, and the check said so before any tree was ever drawn.

Carrying capacity is not a constant anyone typed in. Light capture scales with crown area and maintenance with mass, so the two balance at a finite size and the ceiling falls out of the allometry — which is the actual biological reason trees stop growing. The engine takes a tree from 2 kg to 8.5 tonnes and 30 m over 160 simulated years, then saturates near 31 tonnes and 40 m, having never once built it.

Cost

What a frame can buy

At 20 updates per second a frame is 50 ms, of which physics gets 35. The tempting way to size that budget is bandwidth: 336 GB/s × 35 ms is 11.8 GB, which at 48 bytes per body over ten passes suggests 24 million bodies. That number is wrong, and it is worth saying why, because it is what an optimistic design document would quote.

Tree traversal is not a streaming workload. Each body walks a different path, warps diverge at every opening test, and the cells touched are effectively random in memory. The limit is latency and occupancy. Bandwidth bounds the resident set; it does not bound the step rate.

The budget is not spent on depth. A complete galaxy-to-nucleus chain is 132,400 bodies in 20 MB — one or two percent of a frame. It is spent on breadth: how many regions can be resolved at once. One deep zoom is nearly free; ten thousand simultaneously resolved star systems is what costs.

The single most important line in the cost table is that retarded gravity — the term that makes the whole causal argument legitimate — costs 64% over the instantaneous version. That is the price of the engine's central premise, and it is affordable.

Findings

Four bugs worth recording

Each of these was found by a test or a benchmark rather than by reading the code, and each lived in something that looked entirely reasonable.

  • Mean molecular mass was wrong by exactly 1000× — a units slip routing through Avogadro's number and a gram-to-kilogram factor. It scaled every temperature, pressure, sound speed and Jeans length in the engine. It surfaced as a molecular dynamics test starting at 0.3 K instead of 300 K.
  • The Langevin thermostat re-seeded its noise identically every step. Randomness here is a pure function of an address, which is the point — but an address that omits the step makes "deterministic" quietly mean "identical", and an identical force every step is a constant force. The system heated to 81,000 K against a 300 K target.
  • Stellar burning rates were in cgs while the engine is SI. The standard formulae are quoted in erg g⁻¹ s⁻¹ and g cm⁻³; used raw they made the Sun a hundred thousand times too bright.
  • The conservation metric divided by quantities that legitimately cancel. A hot cloud with little net rotation has particles each carrying angular momentum of order m r v, but the sum nearly cancels — the net can sit 20 orders of magnitude below any individual term, which double precision cannot represent and no finite arithmetic could. Measuring the error against that net reported 100% and meant nothing. Measured against the total angular momentum content, the same code is exact to one part in 10¹³.
The one that was not a bug

A prolongation with two children failed the angular momentum check completely — and correctly. Two point masses have no moment of inertia about the line joining them, so no arrangement of orbital motion can carry angular momentum about that axis. The solver was right to report the system singular; the fix was to give the leftover to intrinsic spin, where it physically belongs.

Limits

What this does not claim

  • The GPU path is designed, not written. Every measured number is single-core CPU; the cost model carries an explicit 60× assumption for the GPU, stated as an assumption and calibrated against reality at runtime.
  • The CPU tree code is 3–10× off production quality. Single-body leaves rather than buckets, per-particle rather than grouped traversal — both standard optimisations, deliberately left out so the reference stays simple enough to serve as a bit-exact oracle.
  • General relativity is post-Newtonian only, adequate to a few hundred Schwarzschild radii. Nuclear and electronic structure are rate- and table-based; chemistry is eight lumped species; turbulence is a prescribed field, not a solved cascade.
  • Structures have no topology and cannot straddle nodes. A body carries no bonds, so materialising a tree and running molecular dynamics on it would let the trunk fall apart — a structure is currently geometry that happens to hold still. And a building spanning several nodes would need cross-links the strictly hierarchical tree deliberately forbids, since that hierarchy is what makes the precision and causal-gating arguments work.
  • The guarantee is about the conserved tuple, not about trajectories. Regenerated detail is a valid sample from the right distribution, not the specific configuration that would have evolved had the detail been tracked continuously. For unobserved matter that is exactly the right standard — it is the one statistical mechanics uses — but it is a weaker claim than "the same simulation, computed lazily", and should not be advertised as the stronger one.
